A composite care table: one table over the fifty conditions, at the grain of the stratum

The five earlier tables joined into one. For each condition, its diagnostic criterion; for each stratum, the share of patients found there, how often they are seen and measured, the level of care, the items of the published risk scores the stratum states, and the treatments that imply it. One knowledge base for the fictitious patient set, for composite risk scoring, and for reading the medication list to the stratum.

Written 2026-10-23 for the operatorCompanion to the standard-care, medication-implied and stratified-care proposals67 new references, 62 retrieved from PubMedDecisions D27 to D34

Nothing described here exists. No file, column, flag, gate or route named below is built. Every row of the condition tables is a draft for a clinician reviewer, not a finding.

Summary

What is proposed. One table whose grain is the stratum, carrying everything the five companions proposed and five things they did not: the diagnostic criterion of each condition and each stratum; the share of the condition's patients at each stratum, with its basis declared; the cadence of visits as well as of measurements at each stratum; the level of care each stratum is managed at; and, on each stratum, the items of the published composite scores it states and the treatments that imply it. Physically it is the six files already proposed, seven new columns, and one generated wide rendering. Nothing is hand-edited twice.

Why one table. The three uses the operator named all read the same rows. A realistic fictitious patient set needs the share of patients at each stratum to draw from, the cadence to date its values, the criteria to keep its values inside the stratum, and the risk-score items to carry a known composite answer. A composite risk score needs the strata because its items are strata. The stratum the medication list implies needs the stratum column on the treatment rows plus one column saying what the presence of a class implies. Three tables would be three places for one fact to drift.

The finding on composite risk scores. Nearly every score a clinician uses before surgery, on the ward or in triage is a sum of items drawn from a problem list, a medication list, a few labs and a few vitals, weighted by a publisher. Their items are not conditions: they are strata. Charlson weights diabetes with end-organ damage twice an uncomplicated one and moderate-or-severe liver disease three times a mild one; the revised cardiac risk index counts insulin-requiring diabetes and a creatinine above 2 mg/dL; the ASA's examples put well-controlled diabetes at class II and poorly controlled at III, dialysis at III and kidney failure without dialysis at IV, an infarction older than three months at III and a recent one at IV; the early-warning score uses a second oxygen scale for the patient whose COPD has reached hypercapnic failure. A problem list without strata cannot compute any of them honestly.

recommendation 1

The generator's known answer first

Every synthetic patient carries the composite scores its strata add up to, so a preoperative patient at an ASA floor of III with a cardiac risk index of 2 and a frailty index in the moderate band can be generated and recovered. No product rule is touched.

recommendation 2

Items with their sources on the page, the sum under D30

Per score, each item the record states with the stratum or value that states it, each it cannot, and the sum with the publisher's class label when every item is stated or declared. Never an outcome rate. The ASA shown as a floor with its reasons, never as the patient's class.

recommendation 3

Medication to stratum by the ladder rule

One column, implies-stratum (exact, floor, none), and a count. Treatment ladders are monotone, so the highest rung present bounds the stratum from below. What the bound means depends on the kind of staging system, which the fifth companion already assigned to every condition.

recommendation 4

Two cadences per stratum

How often the patient is seen and how often each measurement is taken, both in days with the publisher. The page's "none in the window" uses the stratum's cadence when the stratum is known; the generator dates visits and values by them.

Seven columns, and where each lives

  1. diagnosis on the conditions file: the criterion that admits a patient to the condition, as text, with the publisher and year (an A1c of 6.5% or more, a fasting glucose of 126 or more, or a two-hour glucose of 200 or more, each confirmed, ADA 2026 [51]; a post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC below 0.70, GOLD 2023 [73]; two unprovoked seizures more than 24 hours apart, or one with a high recurrence risk, ILAE 2014 [224]). The stratum's criterion already exists on the strata file (defined by), and the two together are what the operator called the diagnostic criteria of each stratum: the condition's criterion admits, the stratum's places.
  2. share and basis on the strata file: the share of the condition's patients at the stratum, as a percentage, and the basis: published with the reference (NHANES, a cohort, a registry), estimate (the drafter's, from the sources named in the row), or none (no figure found; the generator draws uniformly and the row says so). The shares of a condition's strata sum to 100 within rounding, gated.
  3. seen on the strata file: the visit cadence in days at the stratum, from the guideline, with the publisher; and the measurement cadences already on the measures rows keyed by stratum (§6).
  4. care-level on the strata file: one of primary, shared (primary with a specialist), specialist, referral-centre (a tertiary or programme referral the guideline names), urgent (same-day or emergency evaluation), inpatient, palliative; the guideline's own referral rows decide it.
  5. risk-items on the strata file: the items of the published composite scores that the stratum states, as score:item[:weight] tokens from a closed vocabulary file of scores (§5.3): asa:III, cci:diabetes-complicated:2, rcri:insulin, nsqip:diabetes-insulin, efi:ckd, frail:illness, news2:scale2, hasbled:renal. The vocabulary file names each score, its publisher, year, licence and the version of its weights, and the gate holds every token to it.
  6. implies-stratum on the treatments file, beside the fifth companion's stratum and the fourth's implies: exact, floor or none (§7). A row with stratum empty is none by construction.
  7. count on the treatments file, optional: for the ladder rule, the number of classes of a group whose presence together implies the stratum (3 of group H for stage C heart failure with reduced ejection fraction; 3 of group A, one a diuretic for resistant hypertension; 2 of group E for epilepsy not controlled on a first drug).

And one generated artifact: the wide rendering, one row per stratum with every column of every file joined on the condition and stratum keys, written as Markdown for the reviewer and as JSON for the generator and the browser, regenerated by the same target that renders the standard-care table, drift-gated against the files. This is the single composite table the operator asked for, and it is generated, never edited (D27).

The scores vocabulary file

One row per composite score: key, name, publisher, year, reference, the licence as read (free, free with citation, proprietary), the list of its items as the publisher names them with the weight of each in the version named, and for each item where the record can state it: a stratum of this table, a condition of this table, a value (a lab or vital, with the VistA name and the threshold), a medication class, a demographic, the user (the surgery, functional capacity, consciousness), or not in the extract. The risk-items tokens on the strata file must name a score and item this file carries, and the file is the one place a weight is written.

A worked example, type 2 diabetes

Diagnosis: A1c ≥6.5%, or fasting glucose ≥126 mg/dL, or 2-hour glucose ≥200 on a 75 g load, or a random glucose ≥200 with symptoms, confirmed by a second test unless unequivocal (ADA 2026). At or near target: share 50, published (Fang 2021: 50.5% of US adults with diabetes at A1c under 7% in 2015–2018), seen every 180 days, primary care, risk items asa:II cci:diabetes:1 nsqip:diabetes-oral frail:illness efi:diabetes, implied by nothing exactly. Above target without complication: share 25, estimate, seen every 90 days, implied as a floor by a second glucose-lowering agent beside metformin. Markedly above target: share 12, estimate, shared care, asa:III (the ASA's poorly controlled diabetes), implied as a floor by basal insulin started with two or more agents. With atherosclerotic disease, heart failure or kidney disease: a flag that overlaps the band strata, cci:diabetes-complicated:2, set by the problem list and never inferred. Insulin-requiring, hypoglycemia-prone or limited life expectancy: share 13, estimate, asa:III rcri:insulin nsqip:diabetes-insulin, implied exactly by insulin on the list, for the insulin-requiring part only.

The fifty conditions in the composite table

One row per stratum. The fifth companion's columns for the staging system, the stratum's criterion with where VistA holds it, the treatment, the measurements and the studies are not repeated; the wide rendering joins them. These tables draft the new columns only. Diagnosis is the condition's admitting criterion with its publisher, on the condition's first row. Share is the share of the condition's patients at the stratum with its basis. Seen is the visit cadence in days. Care is the level of care. Risk items are the published scores' items the stratum states. Implied by names the treatment whose presence implies the stratum and whether the implication is exact or a floor.

What the table supplies, and what it never does

For a patient, the browser walks the matched conditions and their strata (from the code, or from a served value under D21), collects the risk-items tokens, adds the condition items for matched conditions without a stratum, reads the value items from the labs and vitals with their thresholds, the medication items from the list, the demographic items from the demographics, and lists the user and not-in-extract items as unsupplied. That is a list of items with a source beside each, which is the FDA's fourth criterion applied to a score. The table never reproduces a licensed instrument's items, names an outcome rate, or sums anything the operator has not ruled on.

Preoperative screening

The 2024 perioperative guideline's algorithm branches on urgency, clinical risk, functional capacity and the surgery's risk. The table supplies the risk-index items and the NSQIP inputs the record holds; the user supplies the surgery and the functional capacity. The ASA floor is the table's one genuinely new derivation: dialysis is at least III, an infarction within three months at least IV. Sankar's cohort found 46.7% of elective surgical patients at ASA III and 5.8% at IV, the distribution the generator's preoperative scenario should reproduce.

Care urgency and the level of care

The care-level column is the guideline's own referral rule per stratum: nephrology at G4, an epilepsy centre after two failed drugs, advanced heart-failure therapies at stage D, hepatology at F3–F4, vascular surgery the same week for limb-threatening ischemia, urgent evaluation for a TIA. The highest level across matched strata is shown as a fact, never as a verdict on where the patient is.

Escalation: floor to ICU, local to referral centre

The strata set the baseline (NEWS2's second oxygen scale exists because a hypercapnic COPD patient at 88% is at their baseline) and carry the goals-of-care rows (stage D heart failure, grade 4 COPD, severe dementia, decompensated cirrhosis, castration-resistant cancer), which the ICU admission framework puts before physiology. What the strata do not do is decide: NEWS2's thresholds are the publisher's, and the move is the clinician's.

The VA's own scores

CAN (hospitalisation or death over 4.6 million primary-care patients, C 0.79–0.86), STORM (overdose or suicide among patients on opioids) and the VA frailty index are computed in the Corporate Data Warehouse. None is in VistA's files or reachable through VPR. The product does not compute them and does not imitate them; the page says once that they exist elsewhere.

Cadence by stratum

The guidelines name two intervals per stratum: how often the patient is seen and how often each measurement is taken. The fifth companion put the second on the measures rows by stratum; this proposal adds the first as seen on the strata file, in days, with the publisher. "None in the window" and "last value N days ago, cadence M days" use the stratum's cadence when the stratum is in the record or computed under D21, and the condition's unstaged cadence otherwise, saying which. Nothing is computed from the cadence beyond the age of the last value against it.

Where the cadence depends on the stratum most. The conditions where the two cadences move by an order of magnitude across strata, which the unstaged table gets wrong at one end or the other: chronic kidney disease (eGFR yearly at G1–2, monthly at G5 [63]); heart failure (every 3–6 months at stage C, monthly at D [57]); depression (every 4–8 weeks at mild, every 2 weeks at severe [95]); schizophrenia on clozapine (weekly neutrophils for six months [102]); gout in titration (urate every 2–5 weeks, then every 6 months [67]); hypothyroidism (TSH at 6–8 weeks after a change, yearly when replaced [64]); diabetes (A1c every 6 months at target, every 3 above [51]); COPD after an admission (rehabilitation within 4 weeks [73]); the cancers by phase.

For the generator, a synthetic patient's visits are dated backward from the generation date at the stratum's interval with jitter, each measurement at its row's cadence aligned to a visit where the cadence is a multiple, and the scenario applied per row. The result is a record whose density differs by stratum, the single most visible difference between a realistic synthetic record and a flat one.

The stratum the medication list implies

The fourth companion's inverse reading maps an active medication's class or generic to the conditions the treatments table names it for. With the fifth companion's stratum column on each treatment row, the same lookup returns (condition, stratum) pairs. One column decides what the set means:

  • exact: the class or generic is named at exactly one stratum of the condition and at no lower one, and is given in ordinary practice for nothing else in that condition. Dialysis, an erythropoiesis agent or a phosphate binder (G4–G5); pegloticase (refractory gout); clozapine (treatment-resistant schizophrenia); an anabolic bone agent (very high risk); a biologic for asthma (step 5); home oxygen or non-invasive ventilation (grade 4 COPD); a ventricular assist device or an inotrope (stage D); onabotulinumtoxinA for migraine (chronic); intravenous iron (refractory or severe iron deficiency); intramuscular B12 on a loading schedule (neurologic deficiency); a PCSK9 inhibitor (very high risk ASCVD); dual antiplatelet therapy with a dated event (the first 12 months); docetaxel and kin (castration-resistant); electroconvulsive therapy or esketamine (resistant depression); a long-acting injectable (maintenance schizophrenia); methadone through a programme (moderate–severe OUD); insulin (the insulin-requiring stratum of diabetes).
  • floor: the class is first named at this stratum and continues at the strata above it, so its presence implies this stratum or a higher one: a second glucose-lowering class, a moderate-intensity statin, a preventive for migraine, a daily inhaled corticosteroid, LABA plus LAMA, a bisphosphonate, allopurinol, an alpha-blocker, a cholinesterase inhibitor, lithium, an anticoagulant in atrial fibrillation.
  • none: the class is named at every stratum (an ACE inhibitor in kidney disease, a statin in coronary disease, metformin in diabetes) or at the lowest, which says nothing.

The ladder rule. Treatment ladders are monotone: a guideline adds classes as the stratum rises and rarely removes them. So the number of classes present from a named group bounds the stratum from below, which is the count column: three of the four heart-failure pillars, three antihypertensive classes including a diuretic, triple inhaler therapy, two antianginals and three, two antiseizure drugs, two glaucoma agents, two mood stabilisers with an antipsychotic. The count reads VistA's class and generic names on active medications, and dose where the stratum turns on it (metformin's ceiling at G3b, apixaban's reduced dose, methotrexate above 20 mg, a PPI twice daily).

What kind of stratum a medication implies

  1. A stage of disease (KDIGO, ACC/AHA A–D, Hoehn and Yahr, CDR, Fontaine, METAVIR, AJCC, CDC's HIV stages): stages rarely reverse, so a treatment that is exact or a floor at a stage implies the patient is at that stage or above today. Dialysis means G5.
  2. A class or score of current severity (NYHA, CCS, PHQ-9, GAD-7, ISI, IPSS, IIEF-5, CDAI, ACT, CAT, AHI, GINA's steps): the score moves with treatment, and the medication implies the stratum at which treatment was escalated, not the current one; a patient in remission on a biologic is at the remission stratum. The page says "treatment at the level of CDAI >22 (a biologic with methotrexate)" and never "high activity".
  3. A risk group or a phase (CHA2DS2-VASc, the ASCVD risk tiers, the fracture-risk tiers, provoked against unprovoked, bipolar disorder's phase, the cancers' phases): the medication implies the group: an anticoagulant with atrial fibrillation on the list and no venous thrombosis implies that the score reached the threshold in the prescriber's judgement; an anabolic bone agent implies very high fracture risk; lithium in maintenance implies the euthymic phase only with no acute-episode pattern, which is why the phase strata lean on the count and the dates.

The precedent, and the limit

Von Korff's Chronic Disease Score scored a year's automated pharmacy data by consensus rules over medication patterns and correlated 0.46 to 0.57 with physicians' ratings of disease severity; the VA adapted it as RxRisk-V, 45 pharmacy-defined chronic-disease categories, of which 33 had good-to-excellent reliability and 25 good-to-excellent validity against ICD-9 diagnoses in 126,075 VA users. That is the evidence that medication patterns measure disease burden, and also of the limit: 20 of 45 categories were not valid, and the ones that failed are the ones whose drugs serve several conditions. The stratum inherits that exactly, with one more failure mode: hold-over, the drug started at a higher stratum and continued after improvement, which the kind-2 wording absorbs.

Where it shows

On the medication view, the line per medication gains one clause: "the table lists this class for chronic kidney disease at G4 or above (KDIGO 2024); the problem list carries Chronic kidney disease, stage 3 (N18.3)". The code says G3, the medication says G4 or above, and the reviewer decides whether the code is stale. On the condition frame, the stratum line gains a second source: "stage not in the record; treatment at the level of stage C, reduced ejection fraction (3 of 4 classes present)". The forbidden list grows by consistent with a stage, suggests a stage, staged by treatment; the permitted form is "treatment at the level of".

On the recorded patient 3, the medication list implies no stratum the code does not already state, which is the expected result on a demo patient with five problems and the reason the synthetic set exists.

The stratum is drawn, not chosen

Each condition's stratum is drawn by the share column, so a hundred synthetic kidney-disease patients are 49 at G3, 3 at G4 and 1 at G5 as the global figures say, and a hundred diabetics are 50 at target as NHANES says. Values are inside the criteria

The generator draws the defining value inside the stratum's band and the admitting value above the condition's threshold at some date: an A1c ≥6.5% once in every synthetic diabetic's history; an eGFR under 60 on two dates three months apart; a post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC under 0.70 in a spirometry report's text.

The composite scores are a known answer

For each synthetic patient the generator computes every score the vocabulary carries and writes the items and sums to the patient's README. The round trip asserts that the page's item collection recovers the items. Charlson in both weight versions; the ASA floor with reasons; the frailty index as a deficit ratio with its band; NEWS2 at each time point of the inpatient scenario.

A preoperative patient drawn to Sankar's ASA distribution with a cardiac risk index of 2 and STOP-Bang's record items positive. Adversarial analysis

The case for, made as strongly as the evidence allows

  1. The scores' items are strata, and nothing else in the estate knows them. §5.2 is a table of facts about published instruments, not an argument; a problem list without strata under-counts Charlson, the cardiac risk index, the ASA floor and the frailty indices in a known direction, and the code-based versions of those instruments [178, 182, 183] exist because of it.
  2. One table is the only form in which the three uses agree. The generator, the scores and the inverse reading read the same rows; a second table for any of them is the drift the fourth companion refused.
  3. The share column is what makes a synthetic set realistic, and it is cheap. Twelve of the fifty conditions have a published distribution by stratum in this document; the rest are estimates a reviewer can replace one at a time. Without it the demo set is uniform over strata, which no panel is.
  4. The inference to the stratum has a VA precedent with measured reliability [204], and the ladder rule is the one form of it that is a count of VistA's own class names.
  5. The machinery is small. Seven columns, one vocabulary file, one rendering, four gates, and a generator already proposed.

The case against, made as strongly as the evidence allows

  1. A wide table is unreviewable. Fifty conditions, 175 strata, and now twenty-odd columns per stratum: the standard-care table's "readable in an afternoon" is gone. The answer (narrow files, one generated rendering) is the right one and it means nobody reads the whole; a reviewer reads a condition at a time.
  2. The share column is synthetic data one step from being called measured. Thirty-eight of fifty conditions carry the drafter's estimate; an estimate that sits in a column beside a published figure, in a file a gate accepts, will be cited. The basis column, the rendering printing estimate, and the generator's README are the whole defence, and the global rule says they must be red-proven before the first estimate is committed.
  3. A sum is the first number about the patient no publisher wrote. Option B computes "Charlson 5"; nobody published that the patient's Charlson is 5, only the weights. D21 drew the line at a band over one value, and the operator may hold it there. Option A loses nothing a clinician cannot add in their head, and the generator's known answer is unaffected.
  4. The ASA floor is not the ASA. The class is a judgement with moderate inter-rater reliability (κ 0.61, 67% agreement [197]); the examples improve agreement [196] and do not make the class a formula. A floor derived from a problem list will be read as the class, and the word "floor" will not survive a screenshot.
  5. Hold-over breaks the stratum inference for half the systems. Kind 2 systems move with treatment; "treatment at the level of" is honest and will be read as the stratum. The fourth companion's trial record (wrong where the drug is not specific) applies twice over at the stratum, where the drug's level is even less specific than its indication.
  6. The VA already has better scores. CAN and STORM are fitted models over millions of patients [205, 206]; a hand sum of Charlson in a browser is a weaker instrument shown on a VA patient whose CAN score exists elsewhere. §5.6's one line is the answer and it is a small one.
  7. Escalation scoring is an inpatient tool on a reviewer's desktop. NEWS2 is designed for bedside observation charts every few hours; a page that reads VPR's vitals after the fact is not that, and a demo trajectory that "crosses 7 at hour 31" teaches the wrong lesson about what the product is.
  8. The diagnostic criteria are decoration. The page never applies them (D29), the generator uses only the machine-form bands, and a criterion in prose on a conditions row is reference material that a textbook holds better. Its one defence is that the reviewer of a stratum needs the admitting criterion beside it, and that the generator's "an A1c ≥6.5 once" rule reads it.
  9. The licensed and the proprietary. The NSQIP calculator's model is a web service; the Clinical Frailty Scale is licensed; the MoCA, ACT, IIEF-5 and CAT are instruments the fifth companion already fenced. The scores vocabulary file is one more place to cross the line by accident.

Where each case is wrong

The case for overstates the share column: twelve published distributions and thirty-eight estimates is an estimate table with a published garnish, and its value is the switch between the exhaustive and the realistic draw until a reviewer signs the estimates. The case against treats the sum as a new judgement when it is arithmetic over facts each shown with its source, and the class name as a prediction when it is the publisher's label; the real line is option C's outcome rate, and B stops before it. The ASA objection is right and is answered by the word floor with the reasons listed. The hold-over objection is right and the kind-2 wording is the whole answer; the operator may rule the inference to kind-1 systems only. The escalation objection is right about the product and wrong about the use: the second oxygen scale is the clearest case of a stratum changing a number everyone reads.

Verdict

Build the composite table as the join of the six files with seven columns and a generated rendering, never as a hand-edited wide file. Draw the fictitious set by the share column with every estimate printed as one; date it by the two cadences; carry the composite scores as its known answer, which needs no decision. On the page, show the scores' items with their sources first; sum them with the publisher's class only under D30; show the ASA as a floor with its reasons and never as a class; never show a rate. Infer the stratum from the medication list by exact, floor and the ladder count, with the kind of system deciding what the inference means and the wording saying so.

Limits

One author, one day; the staging papers, the scores' derivation papers and the surveys read in abstract form on 2026-10-23. The share column is the roughest thing in any of the six companions: twelve conditions carry a published distribution by stratum, read from abstracts (the heart-failure stages [207, 208], kidney disease by stage [220], diabetes at target [211], bone density [212], epilepsy's control [215], painful neuropathy [218], chronic pain's impact [217], HIV's continuum [242], obesity's classes [241], hypothyroidism's subclinical share [214], treatment-resistant schizophrenia [156], chronic migraine [219]), and the rest are the drafter's estimates, labelled, for a reviewer to replace. The risk-items column is drafted for the scores named and not for every score a specialty uses. The inference to the stratum is bounded as §7.5 says. The escalation use is a demonstration of a baseline, not a bedside tool. The proposal does not address the transitions between strata, interactions between the scores of several conditions beyond their sums, or pediatric, pregnant or non-adult populations. Nothing here changes the served JSON, the suite, the record check or any VistA.
