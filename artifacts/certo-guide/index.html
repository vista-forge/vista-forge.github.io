Certo

CPRS. In your pocket.

What Certo is

certo Latin, adverb

With certainty; surely; beyond doubt. Certo scio: I know for certain. In Italian and Portuguese the same word still means certain, sure, right.

Certo is that for a patient's record: every value exactly as VistA holds it, read through the same calls CPRS makes, with nothing summarised, inferred or retyped. It lays decades of visits, notes, vitals and labs on one timeline and opens any of it in a tap, so a ten-second look on your phone tells you what CPRS would tell you at a workstation, for certain.

The key feature

A lifelong health record. In your hand.

CPRS spreads a record across tabs, each read in its own date range. Certo's timeline puts all of it on one axis: visits and stays, notes and discharge summaries, every day with vitals and every day with labs, from the patient's first entry in VistA to today.

1988 – 2016 95 visits · 680 notes · 63 vitals days · 44 lab days, on one phone screen

The demo patient here is TEN,PATIENT, the fullest lifelong record in VA's demo VistA system: care in 27 of the 29 years from 1988 to 2016, more years than any of its other 1,305 patients with a record. In CPRS, this demo user's cover sheet lists visits from the last 600 days, which holds none of the patient's 95. The timeline shows all 95, and says so in a line above the lanes.

  • Any scale. 1W 1M 3M 1Y 5Y 10Y All, and a strip of the whole record under them to slide through the years.
  • Find an episode at a glance. Stacks of squares show where care clustered: an admission, a run of clinic visits, a week of labs.
  • Open it in one tap. Tap a square and its notes light up on the Notes page, while every vitals and labs graph shows its reading from that moment.
  • Nothing hidden. Each lane counts what the window shows of all, and how many lie beyond it on either side.

How Certo was made

Built from CPRS itself

Certo earns its name the only way a quick reference can: by being CPRS, not a retelling of it. To VistA, it is CPRS. It was ported from CPRS's own Delphi code, speaks CPRS's own protocol in CPRS's own context, and was checked call by call against CPRS's own traffic. What you know from CPRS holds on the phone, and what VistA enforces for CPRS it enforces for Certo.

  • 75 of 75of CPRS's sign-on, patient selection and alert capabilities matched or ruled out by decision, none left unbuilt
  • 48 of 63of Certo's reads identical to CPRS's own calls, on the wire and in the reply
  • 691 callsof CPRS's own traffic recorded across 141 distinct RPCs, and kept as Certo's tests
  • Side by sidechecked screen for screen against CPRS 1.33 on 29 September 2026

Why that makes it trustworthy

  • Same source, same answer. Certo asks VistA what CPRS asks, so the allergy, the flag and the lab value you read are the ones CPRS would show.
  • Same rules. Your menus, keys and restricted-record warnings apply exactly as they do in CPRS, because VistA sees the same client.
  • Nothing interpreted. Every value is VistA's own text, drawn by code. The only judgements shown are VistA's flags and stored ranges.
  • Provable. Each claim above is a count from recorded CPRS traffic and a side-by-side check, repeatable on demand.

Certo covers sign-on, patient selection, alerts and chart review. Placing orders and writing notes stay in CPRS on a workstation.

Features and functions

Everything below is built and can be tried today. Certo's words for things follow CPRS's, so what you know from CPRS carries over.

Lifelong record

Care timeline
Every visit, stay, note, vitals day and lab day of the patient's life in four lanes on one time axis, at any scale from one week to all of it.
One moment everywhere
Tap a square and the notes, vitals and labs pages all move to that moment. Swipe to move through time; hold, then drag, to read values.
Nothing hidden
Each lane counts what the window shows of all, and how many lie beyond it on either side.

Sign on and session

Sign on
Your own VistA access and verify codes, with your site's notice shown first, as on CPRS's sign-on form. You see what CPRS lets you see.
Division
If you hold more than one division, you choose one, as in CPRS.
Idle timeout
CPRS's own CPRS Timeout window after your CPRS idle time, or the server's limit if that is shorter; at zero you are signed off.
Leaving the app
The screen clears at once. Back within a minute, your patient reopens; after that you are signed off in VistA.

Finding a patient

Search
By name, by the SSN's last four (alone or after the last name's initial), or by the whole SSN.
CPRS's lists
Your default list, Providers, Teams, Specialties, Clinics (with CPRS's date ranges), Wards and PCMM. Save Settings changes your default, as in CPRS.
Last selected, Recent
Your last patient, shared with CPRS both ways, and the patients you opened this session.
Details
CPRS's patient preview before you open the chart: whole SSN, date of birth, location, room-bed, providers, last visit.

Alerts

Notifications
Your pending alerts in CPRS's columns and sort, and the Processed tab for earlier ones.
Process, Remove, Defer
Process an alert as CPRS does (with Open patient where CPRS would take you to the chart), remove one your site allows you to remove, or defer it with CPRS's choices.

The chart

Patient banner
Name, age, sex, SSN last four, ward and bed, primary care, allergies, flags and postings, always at the top.
Flags
Red pills in the banner. The first flag's window opens by itself when the patient opens, as in CPRS.
Latest
Last visit, latest note, latest vitals and the flagged labs of the latest lab day, each with its date and how long ago. A tap opens its page.
Medications
CPRS's Meds tab: outpatient, inpatient and non-VA, in your own CPRS sort, each with its detail; VistA's Medication Reconciliation report.
Problems
CPRS's Problems tab: active, inactive and removed, with codes and detail.
Reminders, postings
The clinical reminders CPRS's cover sheet shows as due, and the postings (crisis notes, advance directives and the like), each opening its text.

Notes

Find and filter
Search titles, authors and places as you type; filter by kind, author, title, place or status; sort by date, title or author.
Text search
Search inside the notes' text, as CPRS's Search for Text does, over up to 100 notes in view.
Reader
The note line for line as VistA holds it, with previous and next through your list.

Labs

Domains
Tests grouped by domain (CBC, Glucose, Electrolytes, Renal, Cholesterol and more). A red dot marks a domain whose latest result VistA flagged.
Trends
Each test a trend against the reference range stored with its results, with VistA's flags in red.
Latest result
The value, its flag, its date and how long ago, at the right of each graph.

Vitals

Nine vitals
BP, HR, RR, Temp, SpO2, Pain, Ht, Wt and BMI, each with VistA's units.
Abnormal limits
Each a trend against your site's abnormal limits, with the latest reading and how long ago.
Chosen moment
With a moment chosen on the timeline, each graph rings its reading nearest that day.

Use case scenarios

Five everyday moments where a phone beside CPRS saves a trip to a workstation.

Pre-rounds on the ward

You want a quick look at each of your patients before rounds, walking the unit.

  1. Open your ward, team or default list on Find a patient.
  2. Tap a patient. The Latest block shows the last vitals and any flagged labs.
  3. Tap the labs line to see the trend; tap ‹ to go back to the list.
  4. Recent keeps everyone you've opened this session, a tap away.

An abnormal lab alert

A notification says Abnormal lab result.

  1. On the alert, tap Process. Where CPRS would take you to the chart, Certo offers Open patient.
  2. Go to Labs. Domains holding a result VistA flagged carry a red dot and open already switched on.
  3. Read the latest value, its flag, its date and how long ago, against the stored reference range.
  4. Opening the chart this way leaves the alert pending.

What happened during that admission?

A patient mentions a hospital stay years ago.

  1. On Timeline, pick All and find the admission square in the Visits lane.
  2. Tap it. Its notes light up in Notes, and Vitals and Labs show the readings nearest that day.
  3. Open the discharge summary and step through the notes with previous and next.

Something written in a note

You remember a note mentioning insulin but not which one.

  1. On Notes, set the window to All.
  2. Change the search's scope from Titles to Text, type insulin, press Enter.
  3. Certo reads each note in view and lists the ones that contain the word: here 1 of 15.

Called away mid-review

You pocket the phone to help with a patient.

  1. The screen clears the moment you leave the app, so nothing shows in the app switcher.
  2. Back within a minute: the same patient reopens with nothing asked again.
  3. Longer: you are signed off in VistA and sign on again.

Walkthrough

From signing on to reading vitals, in the order you'll use it.

1Sign on

  • Open Certo. Your site's notice appears first, above the code fields, as on CPRS's sign-on form. Read it as you would there.
  • Type your VistA access code and verify code, then tap Sign on. You can also type both in the access field as access;verify, as CPRS allows.
  • If you hold more than one division, choose one from CPRS's Select Division list.
  • Any system messages VistA has for you appear next, until you tap OK.
  • Your phone's password manager may offer to save your codes; that is the phone's choice and yours. Certo itself keeps them nowhere.

If your verify code has expired, Certo stops with CPRS's words, You must change your VERIFY CODE at this time. Change it in CPRS; Certo never changes codes. Too many wrong codes lock the device, as they would a CPRS workstation.

2Find your patient

  • Type a name, or the SSN's last four, and tap Find. Each match shows its date of birth with age, sex, SSN last four and ward, so similar names can be told apart before you open one.
  • Or open one of CPRS's lists: Providers, Teams, Specialties, Clinics, Wards, PCMM, or your default list, which opens on arrival.
  • Last selected is your last patient in CPRS or Certo. Opening a patient here makes them your last patient in CPRS too.
  • Tap Details to see CPRS's preview (whole SSN, date of birth, location, providers, last visit) without opening the chart. Tap the name to open it.
  • Your notifications are listed below, with Process, Remove and Defer on each.

3Open the chart

When you tap a patient, Certo asks VistA what CPRS asks, in CPRS's order, before anything from the record is shown:

  • Similar patients. Patients with the same last name and SSN last four are listed in CPRS's window, Please select the correct patient:
  • Restricted records. VistA's warning is shown word for word; VistA logs the access, as it does for CPRS.
  • Date of death. For a patient who has died, CPRS's question comes first, with No as the default.
  • Flags. The first flag's window opens over the chart, narrative first. Close it with ‹.

4The glance

  • The banner stays at the top of every page: name, age, sex, SSN last four, ward and bed, primary care, allergies, flags and the Postings count. As you scroll it rolls up, keeping the name, SSN and flags.
  • Five page buttons sit under it: Glance, Timeline, Notes, Vitals, Labs. Tap the patient's name to come back to the glance from anywhere.
  • Latest gives the last visit, latest note, latest vitals and the latest flagged labs, each with how long ago. A tap opens its page.
  • Medications and Problems are CPRS's Meds and Problems tabs, with the same sections. ⓘ shows more about a row; a tap opens its detail.
  • Clinical reminders and Postings follow, each opening its text.

5The timeline: the whole record at once

  • Four lanes share one time axis: Visits (admissions, appointments, visits; hollow when cancelled or missed), Notes (progress notes and discharge summaries), and a square for each day with Vitals or Labs.
  • Choose a span, 1W 1M 3M 1Y 5Y 10Y All. The strip under it is the whole record: drag its blue dot, or tap the strip, to move the window.
  • Under each lane's name, 45 of 45 counts what the window shows; < 37 at an edge says how many lie that way and moves the window there.
  • Tap a square to choose that moment. The Notes page lights the chosen note (or a visit's notes), and every Vitals and Labs graph rings its reading nearest that day. Tap again to let it go.
  • Swipe sideways to move through time; hold, then drag, to read values across every graph.

The span you choose is shared: the Timeline, Notes, Vitals and Labs pages always show the same window.

6Notes and note search

  • Notes lists every signed note and discharge summary in the window: title, then date, author and place. The note chosen on the timeline is lit.
  • Type in the search to narrow by titles, authors and places as you type. The chips (Kind, Author, Title, Place) filter by VistA's own values, with counts; Date ▼ changes the sort.
  • To search inside the notes, tap Titles ▾, choose The notes' text, type a word and press Enter. Certo reads each note in view (up to 100) and lists those containing it, with a line saying how many it read.
  • Tap a note to read it as VistA holds it, line for line, never re-flowed. ‹ previous and next ›, or a sideways swipe, step through your list.

7Labs

  • Tests are grouped by domain, shown as chips: CBC, Heme, Coag, LFT, Glucose, Electrolytes, Cholesterol, Cardiac, Renal, Pancreas, Endocrine, and Other for anything else. A red dot marks a domain whose latest result VistA flagged; those open switched on.
  • Each test is one row: VistA's name and units, a trend graph, and the latest result with its flag, date and how long ago.
  • The grey band is the reference range VistA stored with the results. A reading above it sits in the top third, one below it in the bottom third. Red is VistA's flag, never Certo's.
  • With a moment chosen on the timeline, each graph rings its reading nearest it and shows that value at the right.
  • A result held as words, or with no range stored, is listed as VistA wrote it at the foot of its domain.

8Vitals

  • One graph per vital, in this order: BP, HR, RR, Temp, SpO2, Pain, Ht, Wt, BMI, with VistA's units under the name.
  • The grey band is your site's abnormal limits for that vital. A vital with no limits stored has a dashed frame and no band, so high on its graph is not "high".
  • Blood pressure is one graph, systolic above diastolic, written as VistA wrote it (120/86).
  • At the right is the latest reading with its date and how long ago. If the window doesn't hold it, it is greyed with not in window.

Safety and security

Certo is built so that what you read on the phone is what VistA holds, about the patient you meant, seen only by you.

The right patient

  • Identifiers under every name in every list, and Details for CPRS's full preview.
  • CPRS's similar-patients check, restricted-record warning, date-of-death question and flag window, in CPRS's order, before the chart shows.
  • The banner, with allergies and flags, stays on screen on every page.

Values you can trust

  • Every value is VistA's own text, drawn by code. No AI model writes, summarises or retypes anything.
  • The only judgements shown are VistA's: its flags and the ranges stored with results. Certo computes none.
  • Every latest reading shows how long ago it was, so an old value never passes for today's.
  • VistA's own words for "nothing" are kept: No Known Allergies is never confused with No Allergy Assessment, and a part VistA could not give says not read, never blank.

Your session

  • Your own codes, in CPRS's context: VistA applies your menus and keys exactly as for CPRS.
  • Your codes are used once to sign on and kept nowhere by Certo.
  • CPRS's idle timeout, and sign-off one minute after you leave the app, whose screen clears at once.
  • Too many wrong codes lock the device, as VistA locks a workstation.

Nothing left on the phone

  • No patient data is stored on the phone; the app caches only its own files.
  • Recent patients live only while the app is open and are forgotten at sign-off.
  • No copying of patient text, no forwarding of alerts, no bulk actions. Those stay in CPRS on a workstation.
  • Certo's server is reached only over a private network, never the open internet.

About these screens

Certo is an independent prototype from the vista-forge project. It is not a VA product, and the VA has not reviewed or endorsed it.

The screens were taken on 29 September 2026 from the Certo web app at a phone's size, showing replies recorded from VA's demo VistA system for its test patient EIGHT,PATIENT. The lifelong record's timeline shows another test patient, TEN,PATIENT, taken the same day from a copy of the demo system made for the purpose, signed on as the same demo user. None of it is a real patient's data. The demo record ends in 2015, which is why latest readings say "11 yr". The notice on the sign-on screen is blurred because the demo system's notice lists its test accounts.
