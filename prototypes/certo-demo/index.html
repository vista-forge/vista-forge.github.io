Demo guide · for physicians

Certo

The Pocket CPRS.

A patient's VistA chart on your own phone, through the same calls CPRS makes. This guide gets you connected, in about fifteen minutes and a short wait while the Administrator sets up your copy, then walks through every main screen.

This demonstration is on a real live VistA system.
All data and patients are fictional.

How it works

Certo runs on a private server holding a copy of VA's public demo VistA system, whose patients are fictitious. The server is not on the public internet. Your phone reaches it through Tailscale, a free app that gives your phone a secure VPN connection to that server and nothing else.

  1. Install Tailscale on your phone and sign in. About five minutes.
  2. Send the Administrator your Tailscale login, by replying to the message that brought you this guide.
  3. The Administrator sets up your own copy of the demo system and replies with an invite link and your demo access and verify codes (never your real VA codes).
  4. Accept the invite with the same Tailscale account.
  5. Open Certo in your phone's browser, sign on with the demo codes, and follow the tour.
  6. When you are finished, tell the Administrator and uninstall Tailscale.

1 · Install Tailscale

  1. Install Tailscale from the App Store (iPhone) or the Google Play Store (Android).
  2. Open it and tap Get Started. When your phone asks to allow a VPN, allow it.
  3. Sign in with your Google, Microsoft, Apple or GitHub account. It is free.
  4. The app shows you as connected. Leave it on.

2 · Send the Administrator your Tailscale login

  1. In the Tailscale app, tap your account, the picture or initial at the top of the screen. Your login is shown there: usually an email address, or something like name@github.
  2. Reply to the message that sent you this guide, with your login exactly as the app shows it. You can copy this and fill it in:

I've installed Tailscale and I'm ready to try Certo. My Tailscale login is: ____________ My phone is: iPhone / Android

The Administrator sets up your copy of the demo system and replies with a Tailscale invite link and your demo access code and verify code.

Certo knows you by this login. Sign in to Tailscale with a different account, and Certo will not find your copy.

3 · Accept the invitation

  1. On your phone, open the invite link from the Administrator's reply.
  2. Sign in with the same account, the login you sent, and accept the invitation.
  3. A machine named minty appears in the Tailscale app's list of machines. It can take a minute.

4 · Open Certo

  1. With Tailscale connected, open Safari (iPhone) or Chrome (Android) and go to https://minty.warg-torino.ts.net:8444/. Type it whole: the full name and the :8444 at the end are both needed. The same link is in the Administrator's reply.
  2. Put it on your home screen, so it opens full screen like an app:
    • iPhone, Safari: tap the Share button, then Add to Home Screen, then Add.
    • Android, Chrome: tap the menu ⋮, then Add to Home screen or Install app.
  3. From now on, open it from its icon. The icon is named Certo; on a phone that added it before 8 October 2026 it is named Loupe, Certo's earlier name. Tailscale must be on.

Tailscale is on when its app shows you connected. Your phone shows it too: VPN in the iPhone's status bar, a key icon in Android's.

The tour

Every screen below is Certo as it stands on 5 October 2026, on TEN,PATIENT, the demo system's fullest record: care in 27 of the years from 1988 to 2016. The patient is fictitious.

5Sign on

  • The screen says Test system, because VistA reports this is not a production account.
  • Type the demo access code and verify code from the Administrator's reply and tap Sign on. Show reveals what you typed.
  • There is no system to choose: Certo signs you on to your own copy, by your Tailscale login.
  • The Site notice under the button is the system's sign-on message, as on CPRS's sign-on form. Tap it to read it.
  • Your phone's password manager may offer to save the codes. Certo itself keeps them nowhere.
  • If alerts are waiting for you, Certo opens on your Alerts; the arrow at the top left takes you to Patient Search.

Too many wrong codes lock sign-on for a while, as they would at a CPRS workstation. Check your codes before trying again.

6Select a patient

  • Search: type a name, or the SSN's last four, in the box under Patient Search and press Search on your keyboard. Try TEN,PATIENT or EIGHT,PATIENT.
  • Each match shows age, sex, birth date and SSN, so like names can be told apart before you open one. Details shows CPRS's patient preview without opening the chart.
  • Browse lists: wards, clinics, teams, treating specialties, primary-care teams and a provider's patients, as CPRS lists them. Your lists holds your own patients and the patients you opened recently.
  • Alerts at the top right says how many alerts you have; tap it to work them. Sign off, in red, ends your session.
  • Tap the patient's name to open the chart. As in CPRS, VistA may first ask about a restricted record or a patient with a similar name, and a patient's flag can open by itself.

7The glance

The chart opens on the glance: what you would look at first, on one page. The five pages are in the bar at the foot of the screen: Glance, Timeline, Notes, Vitals, Labs.

  • The banner, on every page: name, age, sex, birth date and SSN; the allergies; VistA's flags, postings and a fall-risk mark. Tap the identifiers for primary care and how current the data is, the allergies for each one's reactions, a flag or posting to read it. The banner rolls up as you scroll and keeps the name.
  • Latest: the last visit, the next visit, the latest note, the latest vitals and the flagged results of the latest lab day, each with its date and how long ago. Tap a line to go to its page.
  • Medications (outpatient, inpatient and non-VA, as CPRS's Meds tab), Problems, Clinical reminders and Postings follow. Tap a row for its detail. Each section folds with its heading.
  • VistA's flags are red: H or L in red text; a red box means VistA marked the result critical.

8The timeline

The whole record on one time axis, in four lanes: visits and stays, notes, days with vitals and days with labs.

  • Choose how much time to show with the row at the foot: 1W 1M 3M 1Y 5Y 10Y All. It opens on five years. All shows the patient's whole life in VistA.
  • Move through time by swiping sideways on the lanes, by dragging the blue dot on the strip under the spans, or by tapping the counts at a lane's ends (< 39 means 39 earlier).
  • Under each lane's name is how many it shows, of all (56 of 95). The small squares there open a key to the colours: a visit is coloured by kind, and a hollow square was cancelled or missed.
  • Tap a square to choose that moment. Its notes light up on the Notes page. A day with vitals opens that day's readings in the Vitals table, and a lab day opens that day's collection on Labs. Tap it again to let it go.
  • The ⓘ beside the strip shows these gestures again.

9Vitals

  • The range is the chip at the top right. It opens on This stay for an inpatient and Six months otherwise; tap it for CPRS's own list of ranges, or a date range of your own. The demo record's readings end in 2015, so choose All results.
  • A line answers What are they now?: when the latest set was taken and how long ago, how many sets the range holds, and how many readings were outside your site's limits.
  • One row a vital: BP, HR, RR, Temp, SpO2, Pain, Ht, Wt and BMI. Each gives the latest reading with its time and how long ago, a small trend over the range (the grey band is your site's abnormal limits), and the reading before it. Under a row, how the latest reading was taken, in VistA's words.
  • For an inpatient, a line under each row gives that vital's highest and lowest reading in the last 24 hours by VistA's clock, each with its time, and how many readings there were. TEN,PATIENT is an outpatient, so the pictures do not show it.
  • The chips at the foot change the view: Rows; Table, every set as a column, the newest beside the names (swipe the table sideways for earlier ones); and Outside limits, each reading outside your site's limits with the limit it crossed.
  • Tap a row for that vital in depth: a graph with both axes, where a tap reads the reading under it; the latest, the previous, the highest and lowest in the range and how many were outside the limits, each saying how it was found when tapped; then every reading. The row of spans over the graph changes the range.
  • Certo arranges and counts VistA's readings and judges nothing: no arrows, no differences, no words like rising, and no vital in red, since VistA marks none.

10Labs

  • It opens on Latest, CPRS's Most Recent: the newest collection, its tests in the laboratory's own order, VistA's flags in red. Each row gives the result, the result before it with its date, and a small trend against the stored reference range. Step through collections with « Oldest ‹ Previous Next › Newest », as in CPRS.
  • Flagged lists every result VistA flagged, newest first, by collection. By panel groups the tests (CBC, Electrolytes, Cholesterol and more), each with its latest result; a red dot marks a panel whose latest result VistA flagged. Micro lists the cultures; tap one to read the laboratory's report.
  • Find a test by typing part of its name as VistA names it, such as chol.
  • Tap a test for it in depth, as on Vitals, with its reference range drawn and every result with its range and specimen.
  • The range chip sets the span the trends cover; for the demo record, choose All results.
  • Active lab orders, at the top, opens the lab orders not yet complete, as CPRS's Orders tab lists them. CPRS's own Recent Lab Results line for you is at the foot.

11Notes

  • Every signed note and discharge summary, newest first, with no date limit. One row for each title on each day: ×4 means four that day; tap the row to unfold them.
  • Search titles, authors and places as you type. Titles ▾ switches to searching the notes' own text: type a word, press Search, and Certo reads every note, with progress and a Stop button.
  • Date ▾ changes the order. Kind, Author, Title, Place and Status narrow the list.
  • Tap a note to read it, line for line as VistA holds it. previous and next step through the list; the arrow at the left goes back. A note with an assessment and plan opens there, and says so.

12Moving around and leaving

  • Back, on the screen or your phone, goes back one step. From the chart, the arrow at the banner's left goes back to Patient Search and closes the record. Certo never moves from one patient's chart to another's: to see another patient, go back to Patient Search.
  • Hide, in the banner, blanks the screen at once without signing off. Tap it to show the chart again.
  • Leaving the app clears the screen. Back within a minute, your patient reopens; after that you are signed off.
  • After 5 minutes untouched, CPRS's own timeout window counts down; tap Don't Close CPRS to stay.
  • Nothing about a patient is kept on your phone.

When you are done

  1. Tell the Administrator what worked, what didn't, and what you reached for and could not find, by replying to the message that sent you this guide. That is what the demo is for.
  2. Say when you are finished. The Administrator removes your copy of the demo system and stops sharing Certo with you.
  3. Delete the Certo icon from your home screen, and uninstall Tailscale.

Five things to try

  1. A lifetime on one screen. Open TEN,PATIENT, go to Timeline and tap All. Visits, notes, vitals and labs from 1988 on.
  2. One day, its collection. On the timeline, tap All, then a lab day around 2008. Go to Labs: it shows that day's collection, and ‹ Previous and Next › step from there.
  3. Find a note. On Notes, type discharge. Then switch Titles ▾ to the notes' text and search for a word such as cardiology.
  4. What VistA flagged. On Labs, tap Flagged: every flagged result, newest first. Tap one to see its history.
  5. The safety habits. Tap Hide; leave the app and come back; go back to Patient Search and open another patient.

About this demo

Certo reads VistA as a client of its RPC Broker, signed on as you, in the same context CPRS uses, and draws VistA's own text and flags. It computes no judgement of its own. The longer story, with use cases and its safety features, is in the Certo guide. How it was built from CPRS and checked against it is in Building Certo on vista-forge.

The demonstration runs on VA's public demo VistA system, with fictitious patients and demo accounts. You get a copy of it of your own, made for your Tailscale login: what you do there, such as working an alert, no one else sees, and nothing you do reaches the original.

Certo records which screens are opened and how long each answer takes, so it can be improved. That record never holds a patient's name or any of VistA's words.

Questions at any point: reply to the Administrator's message.
